Explainable Text-to-Circuit (X-T2C): staged validation and repair for SPICE circuits

X-T2C maps natural-language circuit specs into SPICE-compatible designs through entity extraction, complexity-aware clarification, component resolution, schema generation, netlist construction, simulation, validation, and explanation. The benchmark compares a staged pipeline against direct prompting across transistor and strict-ngspice circuit families.

Closed-loop generation Extract, resolve, generate, simulate, and repair until the circuit is physically valid.
Direct baseline One-shot schema generation without the staged extraction and validation loop.
What matters Pass rate, physics plausibility, topology fidelity, and component/value correctness.

Key findings

The evaluation favors staged generation when the output must compile, simulate, and preserve circuit intent instead of just looking plausible as text.

Best overall pairing LLaMA-3.1-8B / LLaMA-3.1-70B reached 100.0% pass rate and 0.9973 PPS in the transistor comparison.
Hardest family BJT circuits were much harder than MOSFET switches: 60.0% vs 26.7% pipeline vs direct pass rate.
Most robust family MOSFET circuits stayed near perfect, with 100.0% pipeline pass rate and 96.7% direct pass rate.
Big picture Simulation-grounded repair matters more than one-shot text generation for physically valid circuit synthesis.

Interpretation

  • Validation beats text-only plausibility. A circuit can read well in text while still failing simulation or value constraints.
  • BJT amplifiers are more fragile. They are more sensitive to topology and biasing mistakes than MOSFET switches.
  • The pipeline helps most where the task is hardest. The staged flow is not just safer, it is measurably better in the low-pass and transistor families.
